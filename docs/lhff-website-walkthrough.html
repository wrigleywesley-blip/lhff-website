01 / Introduction

A foundation
that reads like
a publication.

The Foundation isn't a typical non-profit. It crosses creative, spiritual, and conservation lines, and the old site tried to fit that into a brochure layout.

This rebuild approaches it differently. It treats the Foundation like a body of work, not a service. The site reads more like a magazine: chapters, full-bleed imagery, breathing room, a clear voice running through every page.

The original brand stays intact. Cheney's mural opens the home page. The rainbow palette runs through the section dividers. The villa, the studio, the conservation work all get cinematic treatment. Heavy text hides behind accordions so the experience stays light.

NAVID lives directly inside the experience now, not on a separate page. He sits in the corner of every page, available to anyone who wants to talk, whether they're considering a retreat or just reading about the work.

This document walks through the whole thing: the design choices, the page-by-page logic, NAVID's behaviour, what's running under the hood, and what's queued up next.

01 / The brief Section 01

01 / The brief

What we set out to build.

Four things drove this rebuild.

01. A site that feels the way the Foundation feels.

The old site was functional but read like a brochure. The Foundation's work is more textured than that: ceremony, art, conservation, leadership, all running side by side. The new site treats those threads as a publication.

02. Hold the original brand.

Cheney's mural, the rainbow palette, the editorial weight. None of these were up for negotiation. They define the Foundation visually, so they had to define the site visually.

03. Move NAVID into the experience.

Hiding him behind a separate page meant most visitors never met him. NAVID is now a floating presence available from any scroll position, and he greets people based on their relationship to the Foundation instead of assuming they're booked.

04. Light enough for any visitor, layered enough for someone close to the work.

A casual reader, a retreat applicant, an alum, and a partner all need different things from the same site. Accordions, modals, and progressive disclosure let the page be light at first glance and deep on demand.

"Every page should feel like it was made by people who care, for people who care."

02 / The design choice Section 02

02 / The design choice and why

Magazine, not brochure.

Editorial layout

Asymmetric sections, full-bleed images, Playfair Display italic for displays, Open Sans for body, JetBrains Mono for labels. The site reads like a publication, not a services page.

Rainbow as chapter dividers

Each colour signals a movement. Red and orange for love. Yellow and green for happiness. Sky, blue, and purple for freedom. The original brand carries straight through.

Mural as the hero

The site opens with Cheney's mural full-bleed. The Foundation began with the artist, and the home page reflects that.

Accordions for heavy text

The App, the things being built, the roadmap. All hidden by default. Visitors who want depth tap to open. Visitors scrolling get to keep flowing.

Cinema sections

Environment, the villa, the studio. Each gets a full-width treatment. The work is treated like the artefacts it is.

Always a next action

Apply, donate, talk to NAVID, see the app. Visitors are never left guessing what they can do next.

04 / NAVID Section 04

04 / NAVID, the AI companion

A guide that
remembers you.

NAVID sits in the bottom-right of every page. He's a chat companion built into the Foundation's voice.

What he does

  • Greets visitors based on their relationship to the Foundation (curious, considering a retreat, booked, alumni, other).
  • Asks for a first name and a one-line context, so the conversation has a thread.
  • Holds the Foundation's tone: warm, grounded, two to four sentences, ends with a soft question.
  • Recognises common life patterns. Anxiety, sadness, anger, grief, relationships, family, work overwhelm, imposter feelings, big decisions, burnout, stuck.
  • Adjusts retreat-specific topics (schedule, ceremony, integration) based on whether the person is actually attending.
  • Persists state in the browser so the next visit picks up where the last left off.

What he doesn't do

  • Doesn't pretend to be a therapist.
  • Doesn't push the retreat. The retreat shows up only when it's relevant to what someone said.
  • Doesn't store anything off the device. State is local until the real backend is wired.

Why it matters

A chat companion that assumes everyone is on the retreat path is useless to the visitors who aren't. NAVID was built to serve the whole audience: a casual reader, an applicant, an alum looking back. The voice stays the same. The context shifts.

Status modes Curious Considering Booked Alumni Other

NAVID's first message changes based on what the visitor selects in the intake form. A curious visitor never gets retreat-specific language unless they ask for it.

05 / How NAVID responds Section 05

05 / How NAVID actually responds

Calibrated, not generic.

NAVID matches the visitor's input against a set of patterns, then returns a response shaped by the Foundation's voice. The patterns are ordered specific-to-general, so a sentence like "my partner just left me, I feel lost" hits the relationships pattern, not the generic stuck/lost one.

Sample exchange (curious visitor)

N
NAVID
Online · Replies instantly
Hi Sam, I'm Navid. I sit alongside the Foundation as a kind of companion. Ask me anything, or just say what's on your mind today.
My partner just left me. I feel lost.
Sam, I'm sorry. Relationships are where most of our growth shows up, and where most of our wounds do too. What's the part of it that's loudest for you right now?
I think I have imposter syndrome at work too.
Imposter feelings tend to peak when you're growing into something bigger than your old story of yourself. The story is the outdated thing, not you. What's the new story trying to form?

Twenty-plus pattern matchers cover the most common visitor questions. Anything that doesn't match goes to a default response that opens the conversation up rather than closing it down.

06 / Under the hood Section 06

06 / Under the hood

How the site is built.

Stack

Static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework, no build step. Loads instantly, runs anywhere.

Hosting

Vercel. The GitHub repo is connected, so any commit to main goes live in seconds. Zero deploy ceremony.

Design system

Custom, built for this site. Reusable components: chapter heading, marquee carousel, cinema feature, accordion, color block tile, phone mock. Editorial typography throughout.

NAVID, today

A rule-based responder running fully in the browser. Twenty-plus patterns. No backend needed for the current experience to work.

NAVID, when the API lands

The front-end already sends visitor name, intake answers, and full message history to a single endpoint. When the real backend is wired, it slots in without any front-end changes.

POST /api/navid
{ user, history, message }
→ { reply }

Application form

Stores entries in browser local storage today. A quick wire to email or a database when the Foundation is ready.

Mobile

Responsive layout. Editorial sections collapse to single column on phones. The NAVID chat works the same way across devices.

Performance

Asset folder cached for a year. Page weight is light because there's no framework runtime. Cold loads feel instant.

07 / What's next Section 07

07 / What's next

A short roadmap.

The site is live and shareable today. Here's what's queued, in priority order.

Priority 01 · Backend

Wire the real NAVID API.

A Vercel edge function calling the Anthropic API. The front-end is already shaped for it. Estimated half a day. Replaces the rule-based responder with live AI in the same voice.

Priority 02 · Forms

Connect the application form.

Today the retreat application form stores entries in the browser. A small wire-up sends them to the Foundation's email or a Notion database, whichever fits the workflow.

Priority 03 · Domain

Custom domain.

Point the Foundation's domain at Vercel. Five minutes of DNS work. The Vercel URL keeps working as a fallback.

Priority 04 · Mobile polish

Responsive walkthrough.

Walk through every section on a phone. Adjust spacing, image crops, and typography where the editorial layout doesn't translate cleanly.

Priority 05 · Social

Social media carousels.

Built in the same editorial system. Designed for Instagram and LinkedIn. Easily exported as image sets.

Other ideas on the wishlist: a private alumni area where past attendees revisit their integration journal, a press / archive page, and a foundation events calendar.
