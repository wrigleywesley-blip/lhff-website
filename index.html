The retreat companion

Six days that rearrange you. And every day either side.

The companion app for guests of the foundation's retreat, in a private villa above Camps Bay, Cape Town. It carries the forty day preparation, every hour of the six days, the teachings as they are taught in the room, the house itself, and Navid, who is there at three in the morning.

01  The companion

One place for the whole arc.

The retreat is six days. The work is longer than that. The app opens the day a guest is booked and stays open long after they fly home, so nothing has to be remembered, chased, or asked twice.

Forty days before

The preparation guide, stage by stage. The medication gate, the cleanse in two lists, a two week menu plan, a packing list of seventeen things, and one sentence of intention you keep reworking.

Six days at the villa

Every hour your facilitators have planned, the six teachings exactly as they are taught in the room, and the house guide for the fourteen things people actually ask about.

The return

Twelve guided practices filed by the moment you are in, your notes, your journal, and a record you can take out of the app whenever you want it.

It is a progressive web app: Next.js on Vercel, Supabase behind it, and a service worker and manifest, so it installs to the home screen like anything else.

02  Inside the app

Seven rooms, and nothing else to learn.

One tab bar along the bottom, drawn in a single hand. Every glyph is a continuous two pixel line, and every one of them is alive: it draws itself on, as they do here, and in the app it keeps breathing while you are standing in the room.

03  ·  The companion

Navid.

Here with you

Navid is the app's companion, and he is a real conversation, not a scripted one. His replies stream live through the app's own server. He knows the retreat because he reads the same files the screens read, so what he tells you about a ceremony or the Wi-Fi is what the app is showing three tabs away.

Navid · The room

What he knows

The retreat's own material, not a search result.

The ceremonies, the dieta, the villa Wi-Fi, the packing list, the six teachings, the schedule. He is not a general assistant pointed at a help page. He is reading the source the app renders, which means he is never one content update behind the screens.

What he remembers

He asks first, and you can say no.

Before anything else he offers a short intake and waits. Decline it and he simply says you can always do it later, and asks what is on your mind. Accept it and he asks four things:

  • What are you hoping this whole experience gives you?
  • When things get heavy for you, what usually helps, even a little?
  • How has your body been lately, your sleep, your energy?
  • Is there anything you want me to hold on to, so you never have to explain it twice?

Past conversations stay, and you can move between them, so nobody has to start from nothing at two in the morning on day four.

The voice

A call, with something to look at while you talk.

Navid has a voice call mode built around a living orb that moves while he listens and while he speaks, so a spoken conversation has a face to hold. He is written to be spoken: short lines for weight, a held beat where a person would breathe, no markdown or stage directions to stumble over.

He runs three conversational registers. Ordinary talk stays alive, with opinions and callbacks and room to laugh. When something true lands, the jokes stop and he reflects instead. When it is genuinely heavy he goes spare: one sentence, sometimes four words, no questions, no repair.

What he will never do

The honest limits, written into him.

  • No medical advice. Nothing medical, psychiatric, or about medication or dosing. Those go to the retreat's medical team, who are one tap away in the app.
  • No emotion scoring. He responds to feeling in the conversation, always. He will never classify, label, score, or store anyone's emotional state, and never tell you what you are feeling. That is a legal line as much as a design one.
  • No diagnosis, no promises. He does not diagnose, does not promise outcomes, and does not spiritually bypass a hard thing by reframing it.
  • In a crisis he steps aside. If someone is in acute distress he drops every style rule, says plainly that they deserve immediate human support, and points at the on-site facilitator, the medical team, or emergency services.

04  Before you arrive

Forty days, counted down.

The preparation opens the moment a guest is booked. It counts the days to Cape Town on the front of the screen and asks for one thing at a time, in the order the body can actually take it.

One thing before everything else.

If you take any medication or supplement, anything at all, the medical team needs to know now. Some need weeks of lead time, and the team will walk you through it personally, in confidence.

This gate stays lit until you have spoken to them.

I  The month before

Four weeks out, and starting early is fine. Lifestyle changes begin here. Nothing drastic yet.

II  Two weeks out

Days fourteen to eight. The dieta starts: simpler food, no alcohol, quieter evenings.

III  The final week

Days seven to three. The strictest window: no caffeine, no red meat, lighter meals.

IV  The last days

Three days to arrival. Light meals, packing, and as much sleep as you can get.

The cleanse

Two lists, what to avoid and what to eat, with the reason next to each one rather than a rule on its own.

The menu plan

Two weeks of meals, photographed, swap freely. Downloadable as a PDF for whoever does the cooking.

The intention

One sentence you keep reworking until it is yours. It follows you into every ceremony.

05  What is yours

Your journey, kept.

Everything a guest writes belongs to the guest. The export gathers the whole record on the device itself and hands it back, in one piece, whenever it is asked for.

  • It sits on your device. Your notes, your journal, your intention, your checklist, and what Navid holds for you.
  • Sentiment is off by default. The one setting that lets Navid read your recent mood is worded plainly in the app: sentiment only, never your words, off by default.
  • Nothing is scored. No mood scores, no streaks, no leaderboards. The path screen says it out loud: where you are, and what is still ahead. No scores, nothing to keep up.
  • You can take it with you. One control assembles your journey and gives it to you. There is no version of this where you have to ask us for your own writing.
06  Open it

It installs from the browser.

There is no app store listing, and there is no download to hunt for. The app is a progressive web app: open it in a browser, sign in, and add it to your home screen. After that it opens like any other app, full screen, with its own icon.

On iPhone and iPad
  1. Open lhff-v2.vercel.app in Safari and sign in.
  2. Tap the share button in the browser toolbar.
  3. Scroll down the share sheet and choose Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add. The icon appears on your home screen.
On Android
  1. Open lhff-v2.vercel.app in Chrome and sign in.
  2. Accept the install prompt when it appears at the bottom of the screen.
  3. If it does not appear, open the browser menu and choose Install app, or Add to Home screen.
  4. Confirm. The icon appears in your app drawer.

07  The foundation

Love. Happiness. Freedom.

A foundation using fashion, education, spirituality and sustainability to protect human rights, the environment and conservation. Cape Town and Maputo. The retreat and this app are one part of that work, and the part where the foundation's own people are in the room with you.

Love

Reconnection to self, to others, and to the natural world. The starting point of every other pillar.

Happiness

Sustainable wellbeing rooted in presence, not performance. Built through practice, not pursued as an outcome.

Freedom

Liberation from the patterns, fears and conditioning that keep people stuck. The work, and the result of the work.

322 and the rainbow

The digits of 322 sum to seven. Seven is the number of growth, of completion, of arrival, and the mission is to carry this message within seven years. The rainbow stands for diversity, inclusivity, and the shared sky we all live under. Those seven colours run through everything the foundation makes, including this app, where they are used sparingly and on purpose.
