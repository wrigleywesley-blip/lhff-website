Social Credit × WeFree Points: a counter-proposal from the Foundation.

If the world is going to score people, the question becomes who gets to design the scoring. The Foundation has spent eighteen months sketching what a freedom-oriented version of the same architecture could look like. We call it WeFree Points.

The Chinese social credit system has had a strange life in the global press. It is partly real, partly exaggerated, and partly misunderstood. What is harder to argue with is the broader trend it sits inside. Almost every government, every platform, every employer is moving toward some form of behavioural scoring. Insurance, lending, employment, dating, housing. The architecture is being built. The question is what it is built to optimise for.

Most existing systems optimise for compliance. Pay your bills, stay out of trouble, avoid the wrong opinions, your score goes up. The downside risk is well documented. The upside, when it works, is a cleaner consumer credit market and lower friction in everyday transactions.

The Foundation's interest is in the opposite axis. What would a scoring system look like if it optimised for freedom rather than compliance? Not freedom in a libertarian sense. Freedom in the sense the Foundation uses it. Liberation from the patterns, fears, and conditioning that keep people stuck.

WeFree Points, in plain language

The proposal is provisional, sketched, and openly speculative. It is published as research, not as a product. Three principles sit at the centre.

First, the score belongs to the person, not to the institution. Whatever is measured, the individual holds the record, and chooses what to share with whom. This inverts the existing model, where the score sits with the institution and the individual sees a number.

Second, what gets measured is what the person says matters. A WeFree Point is awarded for actions the individual has identified as part of their own growth. Daily practice. Honest conversations. Repaired relationships. Time off the phone. The system's job is to mirror, not to instruct.

Third, the score is private by default and useless to third parties. There is no way to compare two people's WeFree Points. There is no leaderboard. There is no employer dashboard. The points exist for the person to see themselves more clearly, not for anyone else to see them.

What this is, and is not

It is not a real product. It is a thought experiment turned into a working sketch. We have built a small prototype inside the LHFF App for retreat alumni, who use it to track their own daily practice. The data never leaves the device. The Foundation does not see it. There is no analytics layer.

It is also not a critique of any specific national system. The criticism that needs writing about state-run social credit has been written, repeatedly, by better-placed voices. Our work is the constructive sketch, not the critique.

Why a foundation is doing this at all

Two reasons.

First, because the technology of behavioural scoring is going to be built whether or not it is well thought through. We would rather contribute a working alternative than wait for the default to land. The default will be designed by the entities with the most to gain from compliance. We are not those entities.

Second, because the Foundation's whole mission is the inner work. If software is going to play a role in how people relate to themselves, we want a clear voice in what that software does. WeFree Points is one way of putting that voice on paper.

What happens next

The prototype will continue to be developed inside the LHFF App for our cohort. The longer-form paper, with the architecture, the privacy model, and the ethical frame, will be published in 2026. We will also be talking to a small number of academic and civil-society partners about whether any of this is worth scaling beyond the Foundation's own membership.

If you work on identity, privacy, behavioural design, or post-social-credit architectures and want to compare notes, write to us. The work is exploratory. It improves with collaborators.
