The retreat in plain language Buddha Villa Retreat · 03

What this is

A six-day residential retreat in Cape Town.

The Buddha Villa Retreat is a six-day residential programme held at a private villa in Camps Bay, Cape Town. Twelve guests at a time. Practitioner-led. Built around three guided plant medicine ceremonies, woven into a structure of teaching, rest, conversation, and integration.

It is held with the seriousness of a clinical programme and the warmth of a private retreat. Every guest is screened. Every ceremony is held by experienced facilitators. Preparation begins weeks before arrival, and integration support continues long after departure.

Not a wellness break. Not a workshop. A contained, professionally facilitated environment for deep personal work. People come here to do something specific, then leave with a structure for living what they found.

01 · The questionBuddha Villa Retreat · 07

The first journey

If you came
for clarity.

The fog is the problem. You have outgrown a chapter and can't yet see the next one. A decision is waiting. A direction is asking to be named. A purpose feels close, but not close enough to hold.

The clarity journey is about cutting through. Ceremony tends to bring it sharp and quiet. You leave with fewer questions, not more answers, but the questions you keep are the right ones.

What the journey often surfaces

  • A direction you'd been avoiding naming.
  • A truth about a relationship or commitment.
  • A purpose that has been there all along.
  • A decision the body already knew.
01 · The questionBuddha Villa Retreat · 08

The second journey

If you came
for healing.

Something is held in the body that the mind has not been able to move. Grief that did not get to grieve. A relationship that did not get to be named. A version of yourself you had to abandon to survive.

The healing journey is slower and quieter than people expect. Ceremony does not push. It opens, and what needs to come up comes up. The work is in being present for it, and being held while it moves.

What the journey often surfaces

  • A grief never properly named.
  • A pattern in relationships that keeps repeating.
  • A part of yourself you have not forgiven.
  • An old fear the body is finally ready to release.

The signal matters more than the label. What you tell us in screening shapes how your week is held, the support you are paired with, and the integration we offer afterward.

03 · The medicineBuddha Villa Retreat · 19

In plain terms

What we mean by plant medicine.

Plant medicine is a plant-based preparation, used inside guided ceremony, to support the kind of inner work most people never have a structured way to do. It has a long lineage, far older than any modern wellness movement, and it is treated with the seriousness of that lineage at this retreat.

Used properly, it can dissolve the defences a person has spent decades building, and let them see clearly what is underneath. It does not give you answers. It removes what was in the way of seeing them.

It is not recreational. It is not a shortcut. It is a tool, held inside a framework, used with care.

Plain-language facts:
– Taken once per ceremony, three ceremonies over the week.
– Always with experienced facilitators present.
– Each guest is medically and psychologically screened before acceptance.
– No phone, no work, no outside distractions during ceremony.
03 · The medicineBuddha Villa Retreat · 20

The approach

A doorway.
Not a destination.

The medicine does the opening. The work is what you do with the door once it is open. That work happens in the teaching, the fire circles, the silence, and the integration that follows. The ceremony is one part of a much larger container.

This is why the Foundation builds the retreat around the medicine, not in service of it. Preparation, teaching, rest, conversation, integration. All of these are weighted equally with the ceremonies themselves.

The medicine doesn't give answers. It removes what was in the way of seeing them. The seeing is yours to do.
03 · The medicineBuddha Villa Retreat · 21

What actually happens

A ceremony, in practical terms.

For most corporate guests, this is unfamiliar territory. Here is what a ceremony actually looks like at the Buddha Villa.

01
A held indoor space
A purpose-prepared room with mats, blankets, pillows, and soft light. Each guest has their own space. No phones, no clocks, no outside contact.
02
The medicine is taken at the start
Guests take the medicine seated, in silence, in turn. Then they return to their space. The effect builds over the next hour.
03
Music guides the journey
Live or curated music carries the experience for several hours. Most guests spend the time lying down with eyes closed, moving through the inner work.
04
Facilitators are present the whole time
Experienced practitioners stay in the room from start to finish. They support each guest individually as needed. No one is alone with the experience.
05
Morning after — integration circle
A held conversation the next morning. Guests share what came up if they wish to. Practitioners help frame what was felt into something a person can carry forward.
03 · The medicineBuddha Villa Retreat · 22

Safety

How the space is held.

Plant medicine is only as safe as the container around it. The Foundation builds that container deliberately, in four layers.

01
Screening before acceptance
Every guest completes a medical and psychological screening. Certain conditions and medications make the medicine unsafe. The Foundation says no when it isn't right.
02
Experienced, professional facilitation
Ceremonies are led by practitioners with years of experience holding this work. The team includes medical support on call for the duration of every ceremony.
03
A small, known cohort
Twelve guests, no more. Small enough that the facilitators know what each person is carrying, and can hold accordingly.
04
Framework before and after
Preparation in the weeks before. Integration in the weeks after. The ceremony sits inside a structure, never on its own.

05 · The arc beyondBuddha Villa Retreat · 30

Before you arrive

Preparation begins weeks out.

What a guest brings to the ceremony shapes what the ceremony can do. So the work starts before arrival. In the weeks leading up, guests are guided to slow down: eat lighter, sleep more, sit quietly, pay attention to what they are carrying.

The Foundation provides a structured preparation guide, plus access to NAVID (the Foundation's AI companion) for the lead-up.

Preparation is the first ceremony. The week at the villa simply continues what the preparation begins.
05 · The arc beyondBuddha Villa Retreat · 31

After you leave

Integration is where
the lesson becomes a life.

Guests leave with an integration plan: slow mornings, journaling, breathwork, walks, conversations. The ceremony opens the door. Integration walks you through it. This is where the real change holds or fades.

Integration is supported by the Foundation for as long as the guest needs it. Through scheduled check-ins, through community, and through the technology layer described on the next pages.

The Foundation does not disappear on Friday. Support carries through the months after the retreat.

NAVID

An AI companion,

Every retreat guest gets access. NAVID supports preparation before arrival, holds the space between sessions during the week, and walks alongside through the weeks of integration after. It remembers conversations. It calibrates to where you are.

It is locked. Only retreat guests and Foundation members have access.
