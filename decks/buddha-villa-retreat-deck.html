01 · The question Buddha Villa Retreat · 05

Sit with this for a moment

Are you looking for
clarity,
or healing?

Most people come for one of the two. Most discover, somewhere in the week, that they came for both.

The answer is not a label. It is a signal. It indicates a lot about how the journey will unfold for you, what the ceremonies will surface, and what integration will ask of you on the other side.

01 · The question Buddha Villa Retreat · 06

The first journey

If you came
for clarity.

The fog is the problem. You have outgrown a chapter and can't yet see the next one. A decision is waiting. A direction is asking to be named. A purpose feels close, but not close enough to hold.

The clarity journey is about cutting through. Ceremony tends to bring it sharp and quiet. You leave with fewer questions, not more answers, but the questions you keep are the right ones.

What the journey often surfaces

  • A direction you'd been avoiding naming.
  • A truth about a relationship or commitment.
  • A purpose that has been there all along.
  • A decision the body already knew.
01 · The question Buddha Villa Retreat · 07

The second journey

If you came
for healing.

Something is held in the body that the mind has not been able to move. Grief that did not get to grieve. A relationship that did not get to be named. A version of yourself you had to abandon to survive.

The healing journey is slower and quieter than people expect. Ceremony does not push. It opens, and what needs to come up comes up. The work is in being present for it, and being held while it moves.

What the journey often surfaces

  • A grief never properly named.
  • A pattern in relationships that keeps repeating.
  • A part of yourself you have not forgiven.
  • An old fear the body is finally ready to release.
01 · The question Buddha Villa Retreat · 08

The two journeys

In time, the two become the same.

Most who come for clarity find that the fog was a wound. Most who come for healing find clarity waiting on the other side of the work.

You do not need to know which one you are here for. You only need to be honest about which one you started with. The medicine and the framework will hold the rest.

The signal matters more than the label. What you tell us in screening shapes how your week is held, the support you are paired with, and the integration we offer afterward.

The Atlantic on one side.

03 · The medicine Buddha Villa Retreat · 14

The approach

Plant medicine is a doorway.
Not a destination.

The Foundation treats plant medicine with the seriousness it deserves. It is not recreational. It is not a shortcut. It is a tool that, held properly, can dissolve the defences a person has spent decades building, and let them see what is underneath.

The medicine does the opening. The work is what you do with the door once it is open. That work happens in the teaching, the fire circles, the silence, and the integration. The ceremony is one part of a much larger container.

The medicine doesn't give answers. It removes what was in the way of seeing them. The seeing is yours to do.
03 · The medicine Buddha Villa Retreat · 15

The container

How the space is held.

Plant medicine is only as safe as the container around it. The Foundation builds that container deliberately, in four layers.

01
Screening before acceptance
Every guest is screened for medical and psychological fit. The medicine is not right for everyone, and the Foundation says so when it isn't.
02
Experienced facilitation
Ceremonies are led by experienced practitioners. Guests are never alone with the experience. Support is present through every hour.
03
A small, known cohort
Twelve guests, no more. Small enough that the facilitators know what each person is carrying, and can hold accordingly.
04
Framework before and after
Preparation in the weeks before. Integration in the weeks after. The ceremony sits inside a structure, never on its own.

A day in the rhythm

The hours between the ceremonies.

06:30
Sit before the day arrives.

Twenty minutes of stillness. No phones.

08:00
Yoga and movement.

Bodywork before the mind shows up.

10:00
Teaching, a pillar in depth.

Group work. Real conversations.

13:00
Long table lunch.

Food cooked at the villa. The community forms here.

15:00
A long walk, mostly in silence.

The morning's work settles.

Fire circle, or ceremony.

Depending on the day. The centre of the week.

Rest is not a gap in the programme. It is part of the programme. The work needs space to land.

By the third day, most guests stop checking the time. The rhythm carries them. That is the point.

04 · The journey Buddha Villa Retreat · 20

The three ceremonies

Each one goes further than the last.

01
Monday — meeting yourself
The first ceremony is about contact. Most guests meet a version of themselves they have been avoiding. The facilitators hold steady. Nothing is forced.
02
Tuesday — moving through it
The second ceremony builds on the first. With the door already open, the work goes deeper into what the first ceremony surfaced. Often the hardest night, and the most important.
03
Wednesday — letting it settle
The third ceremony is held with the most care. It is less about opening and more about integration, about beginning to carry what was found back into the body.

05 · The arc beyond Buddha Villa Retreat · 23

Before you arrive

Preparation begins weeks out.

What a guest brings to the ceremony shapes what the ceremony can do. So the work starts before arrival. In the weeks leading up, guests are guided to slow down: eat lighter, sleep more, sit quietly, pay attention to what they are carrying.

The Foundation provides a structured preparation guide, and a companion, in the weeks before.

Preparation is the first ceremony. The week at the villa simply continues what the preparation begins.
05 · The arc beyond Buddha Villa Retreat · 24

After you leave

Integration is where
the lesson becomes a life.

Guests leave with an integration plan. Slow mornings, journaling, breathwork, walks, conversations. The ceremony opens the door. Integration walks you through it. This is where the real change holds or fades.

NAVID, the companion.

Every guest gets access to NAVID, the Foundation's companion. Trained on the seven pillars and the rhythm of a retreat, it holds the space between sessions, through preparation, and long into integration.

The Foundation does not disappear on Friday. Integration support and the companion carry the work for as long as the guest needs it.
