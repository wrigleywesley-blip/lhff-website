02 · The medicine Buddha Villa Retreat · 06

The approach

Plant medicine is a doorway.
Not a destination.

The Foundation treats plant medicine with the seriousness it deserves. It is not recreational. It is not a shortcut. It is a tool that, held properly, can dissolve the defences a person has spent decades building, and let them see what is underneath.

The medicine does the opening. The work is what you do with the door once it is open. That work happens in the teaching sessions, the fire circles, the silence, the integration. The ceremony is one part of a much larger container.

03 · The journey Buddha Villa Retreat · 11

The three ceremonies

Each one goes further than the last.

01
Monday — meeting yourself
The first ceremony is about contact. Most guests meet a version of themselves they have been avoiding. The facilitators hold steady. Nothing is forced.
02
Tuesday — moving through it
The second ceremony builds on the first. With the door already open, the work goes to what the first ceremony surfaced. This is often the hardest night, and the most important.
03
Wednesday — letting it settle
The third ceremony is held with the most care. It is less about opening and more about integration, about beginning to carry what was found back into the body.
Between every ceremony: a full day of teaching, rest, movement, and held conversation. The space between the ceremonies is where the medicine becomes meaning.

04 · The arc beyond Buddha Villa Retreat · 14

Before you arrive

Preparation begins weeks out.

What a guest brings to the ceremony shapes what the ceremony can do. So the work starts well before arrival. In the weeks leading up, guests are guided to slow down: eat lighter, sleep more, sit quietly, pay attention to what they are carrying.

The Foundation provides a structured preparation guide, and NAVID, the Foundation's companion, walks alongside each guest through the lead-up.

The preparation work
  • Slow the body down. Lighter food, more sleep, less stimulation.
  • Sit quietly with yourself, ten minutes a day.
  • Notice what you are carrying in. Name it before you arrive.
  • A structured guide, plus NAVID, in the weeks before.
Preparation is not optional. It is the first ceremony. The week at the villa simply continues what the preparation begins.

The cohort Buddha Villa Retreat · 16

Who it's for

Twelve people ready to stop avoiding the work.

This is for you if
  • You have done some inner work and hit a wall the usual tools can't move.
  • You carry something old that you are finally ready to look at.
  • You can give six days fully, with preparation before and integration after.
  • You are willing to be held, not just to perform being fine.
This is not for you if
  • You are looking for a holiday, an escape, or an experience to collect.
  • You cannot commit to the preparation and integration around the week.
  • Screening surfaces a medical or psychological reason it would not be safe.
  • You want the door opened, but not the work of walking through it.
Every applicant is screened. Saying no to the wrong fit is part of how the Foundation keeps the container safe for the right one.
